Ross 458 C: Gas Giant or Brown Dwarf?

Ross 458 C is a widely separated planetary-mass companion, 1100 AU from its host binary Ross 458 AB. Its composition is a record of where and how it formed — so we measured it.

What the spectrum told us

We obtained high-resolution (R ∼ 2700) emission spectra of Ross 458 C with JWST NIRSpec Fixed Slit in the F070LP, F100LP, and F170LP filters.

We fit these spectra to the Sonora Bobcat, Sonora Elf Owl, and ExoREM forward models, and retrieved atmospheric properties using the POSEIDON retrieval code. The forward model grids preferred enhanced metallicity relative to the host, while the retrieval preferred a metallicity consistent with the host within 1σ.

The retrieval provided a much better fit to the spectrum, so we inferred that Ross 458 C likely formed through fragmentation of the molecular cloud that formed its host — more like a star that came out small than a planet built in a disk.

Demographic Model

A companion population model fit to the orbital separations and mass ratios of gas giants and brown dwarfs, giving the expected number of each per star as a function of host mass, companion mass, and orbital separation.

The model treats gas giants and brown dwarfs as two overlapping populations: a log-normal distribution in orbital separation for each, paired with a power-law mass-ratio distribution for giant planets (and a flat one for brown dwarfs, which we treat as an extension of the stellar companion population). The brown dwarf orbital separation parameters are fixed for each spectral type to literature values.
